Mappings for the player pronoun thing \ppr[X][Y] in different languages follow.
These are listed in the order M, F, N like in the anPlayerPronoun method.

========================
ENGLISH
========================
X = 0:
- Y = 0: "is", "is", "are"
- Y = 1: "s", "s", ""
X = 3:
- Y = 0: nominative pronouns "he", "she", "they"
- Y = 1: accusative pronouns "him", "her", "them"; should also be used with reflexive as \ppr[3][1]self, as although "them" is technically plural, it is singular here and "yoursel(f/ves)" implies "themsel(f/ves)"
- Y = 2: possessive pronouns "his", "her", "their"
- Y = 6: possessive adjectives "his", "hers", "theirs"; entirely since \ppr[3][6] is not \ppr[3][2]s if the player is masculine

No other X values are necessary, as 3rd person plural is always "them" and 1st and 2nd person don't decline for gender.
Yes, technically \ppr[3][6] is .FOR.GEN, but with no T-V distinction in modern English, I'm reusing it for this

========================
SPANISH
========================
X = 0:
- Y = 0: "o", "a", "e" (related endings that vary only in these vowels, such as -it@, are unnecessary, just do <word>it\ppr[0][0])
- Y = 1: "dor", "dora", "dore" (unsure if this is standard)
X = 3:
- Y = 0: "él", "ella", "elle"
- Y = 1: "lo", "la", "le"
X = 4, Y = 0: "nosotros", "nosotras", "nosotres"
X = 6, Y = 0: "ellos", "ellas", "elles"
X = 6, Y = 1: "los", "las", "les"

X = 3 and 6, Y = 2 is technically excluded because it is always "a \ppr[X][0]" (but because first and second person are different, \ppr[X][2] is defined)
X = 3 and 6, Y = 3 is excluded because it is always either "a \ppr[X][0]" or "le" (but because first and second person are different, \ppr[X][3] is defined)
X = 4, Y = 1 is excluded because it is always "nos"
X = 7 and 9 are excluded because it is always the same as either X = 4 or 6
X = 2 and 5 are excluded because although formal/informal pairs exist in \ppr, they are unnecessary, and also vary by region
No verb endings are necessary for the same reason

========================
ROKADONG
========================
There are far too many pronouns to include every case; just use "<particle><pronoun>" where <particle> is the case particle and pronoun is one of the following (assuming it varies by gender)

X = 2:
- Y = 0: "ryu" in all cases
- Y = 1: "zeda", "zenaz", "zerav"
- Y = 2: "tau" in all cases
- Y = 3: "talah" in all cases
- Y = 4: "ludi", "lunu", "luvi"
- Y = 5: "javital" in all cases
X = 3:
- Y = 1: "landa", "lanaz", "lanil"
X = 6:
- Y = 1: "landa razil", "lanaz razil", "lanil razil"

X = 1, 4, and 7 are excluded because they do not vary by gender of the referrent
X = 2, Y = 0, 2, and 3 are deemed unnecessary because "ryu", "tau", and "talah" don't vary by gender, but they are still present
X = 5 is excluded because they are always "zenyaci", "\ppr[2][Y]yaci" for Y <= 2, or "\ppr[2][Y] tékuha" for Y >= 2
X = 6 is deemed unnecessary because the neuter form is generally always used, but this isn't true of all speakers
X = 8 and 9 are excluded because it's always either the masculine or neuter form of a X = 5 or 6 respectively
